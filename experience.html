00

What is our experience like?

First, explained through Bella's perspective.

The easiest way to explain my experience is that growing up, I started separating different parts of my emotions into named 'splits' (compartmentalization).

When I was really young, I was dealing with a lot of bullying, fear, pain, and things I didn't know how to process. To cope, I started naming those emotional 'clusters'. The first one was Jinx, who held a lot of my anger, hurt, resentment, and pain.

Over time, life got harder and I needed different ways to cope. So more splits formed. Dylan became stability and calm when everything felt overwhelming. Jake became the part of me that held onto childhood happiness and empathy. As years went on, I kept organizing different emotions, experiences, memories, and coping mechanisms into these named parts.

To me, they were never random characters or imaginary friends. They always felt like parts of me. That's why I call them 'splits.' They're different clusters of emotions, memories, and experiences that my brain organized separately.

Before I ever learned about DID/OSDD1, systems, alters, or any online communities, I already experienced myself this way. We called our internal world the Mindscape, and we never really used system terminology.

I don't know exactly what a professional would call my experience yet, and I'm still hoping to get assessed one day. I'm not attached to getting a DID diagnosis. What matters to me is understanding what this experience is from a professional standpoint.

What I do know is that the experience itself is real. I've lived with it since childhood. Whether it's ultimately called OSDD, another dissociative condition, or something else entirely, I know these parts have helped me cope with things I couldn't handle alone.

Side note: They are still distinct personalities sharing my body. Nothing I've said changes that.

The only google definition that's actually a bit close to our experience.

OSDD-1 (Other Specified Dissociative Disorder subtype 1) is a diagnosable mental health condition where a person has symptoms similar to Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) but does not meet all of its official diagnostic criteria.It was previously called DDNOS-1 (Dissociative Disorder Not Otherwise Specified). It is split into two common unofficial, descriptive subcategories: OSDD-1a and OSDD-1b.

Key Subtypes OSDD-1a: Features less distinct or less differentiated identity states (alters), but the person usually experiences dissociative amnesia (memory gaps or lost time) between switches.

OSDD-1b: Features distinct and separate identity states, but the person does not experience significant amnesia or loss of time when a different part takes control.

From these, we believe we lean toward OSDD-1b (unnoficial term. Keep that in mind.)

But at the end of the day, it doesn't matter. Our experience is still ours and no one can really... comment on what we go through. You don't have our brain, and we can't make you see the world through our eyes.

01

Dissociation

Dissociation is a bit weird for us.

We don't get the CRAZY system amnesia most other systems get. Surprisingly, it's more common for us to be pretty aware of a lot that's happening if we are co-fronting while a different member is in front.

For example, if Ares is co-fronting, and Hex is fronting, He's going to be fairly aware of what Hex is doing, but it can get blurry and confusing for Ares at times.

It's like looking through a window that's slightly foggy. You can make out whats happening for a while, but when it gets too foggy, you start getting less and less aware of what's happening on the other side of the window.

However, we do have issues of memory loss, and sometimes black outs.

02

Switching

Swithcing for us can happen pretty suddenly.

Other times it can be almost... like water slipping through fingers.

Think of it like clay. You get all sorts of colours. Some bits of clay are bigger than others

When you start pressing different colours of clay together, they get harder to take apart.

We're like clay. Minus the appetising smell and salty ass taste.

  • Co-Fronting

    04

    Co-consciousness

    When 2 or more members are co-concious, all parties are fully aware of what's happening.

    It's similar to being blended. When one of us is blended with one or more members, it's fairly the same, just it becomes easier for multiple splits to control the body at once, and it gets really weird.

    When there's co-conciousness, it's mostly thoughts that get extremely messy, so we resort to talking to eachother over notes or text until it's over and we can communicate internally again.

    I'll explain this better when I'm less tired.

    05

    Memory

    Memories being shared or not depend HEAVILY on the splits.

    since we do not all know eachother, we do not all know every single thing.

    Clusters of splits (like Aether, Leo, and Dimitri) will share memories because they formed together.

    Some of us only remember up to 2024

    Some of us cant remember anything before last year (2025).

    There are gaps, partial memories, different perspectives, things scattered across notes some of us dont even remember where to find.

    But it's not as simple as, "I don't remember that cause I'm forgetful". No.

    06

    Internal experience

    Our internal world is called the Mindscape. We used this term for years before learning that many systems use 'headspace'. 'Mindscape' simply feels like home to us.

    We actually experience this internal world as... literally just a house.

    Some random minimalist house, with a living room that can see into the kitchen. And the bedrooms are the only things actually insane.

    For example. Sin and Hex's rooms are quite literally in the dungeon.

    Why do we have a dungeon you ask?

    I don't know. Jake made it. He had a wild imagination that day and Sin claimed a cell first. This was before we got Hex.

    07

    Communication

    We communicate with eachother in a lot of ways!

  • Internally - Literally just talking to eachother in our head.
  • Through notes and our diary
  • On discord when our head gets really loud. We made separate discord accounts for this reason.

    • Sometimes whoever is fronting could be completely peaceful, making an egg, and then randomly someone in co-front or a different front might randomly yell; "FUCK YOU" and start an argument.

    One time I (bella) Was making chicken schintzels, and Ace randomly started an argument because he wanted us to learn the moon walk. It sounded like a family fued. It was stupid.

    08

    Splits

    This is simple really. "Splits" is literally just our own term for "Alters". We didn't know what it was before so we made our own terminology and it stuck.

    Especially these days when the world is full of people who fake it. We just keep the different terms so we arent boxed in with them.

    09

    Daily life

    It can get in the way of family life if the wrong split is fronting. Or we can handle it really well by masking.

    There's no guarantee whether something good or bad is going to happen, because we cant predict who is going to front and when. Either the people in our life dont notice or just assume it's mood swings.

    It's preferable that way.

    10

    Things people misunderstand

    People often assume we're possessed (which is wrong). or pretending. Or misled.

    Because apparently it's impossible for us to have trauma, but they're allowed to.

    Gotta love double standards.

    It's also common for people to block us when we open up about being a system. We've gotten used to it by now.

    A NOTE FROM US

    I need you to remember, that this is OUR experience. This is how WE experience life as a system and it is not going to be the same for everyone with this disorder.

    It's also good to keep in mind that it's not fun, and it can be extremely stressful to live like this. We aren't glamourising it, we're just making known what we go through in the simplest way we can.
